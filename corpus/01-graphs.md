# What is a knowledge graph

A knowledge graph models information as nodes connected by edges. Nodes
represent entities (people, documents, concepts), and edges represent the
relationships between them.

Unlike a relational database, where facts live in tables and joins
reconstruct relationships at query time, a knowledge graph stores the
relationships directly. This makes multi-hop questions — "who knows
somebody who wrote a paper about X?" — naturally cheap.

Knowledge graphs excel when the shape of the data is not known in
advance, when relationships are first-class, and when traversal patterns
vary widely across queries.
